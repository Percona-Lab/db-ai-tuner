## 2. #125 ibench l.i1 steady (945b4ca, budget 9) and the change-buffering A/B

### Limits and hypothesis outcomes

The workload is **flush-bound on a saturated disk**:
- Disk util 96-97% at 3.4 ms await, dirty pages 93% against a 90% limit, checkpoint age 76%, PSI io 24%.
- Diagnosis: io-flush, redo, sched-delay, swap-cold.
- Breakdown: CPU (not profiled) 60%, data-file I/O 30%, redo 9%.
- No CPU profile was taken: the build gated profiling on CPU ≥ 60%, and mysqld was at 31%.

| Run | Experiment | Hypothesis outcome | Right? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | best-practices | refuted, −0.8% | yes; no evidence linked it to the bottleneck |
| 4 | redo-grow 7.5G → 32G | supported: data-file I/O per trx −25%, checkpoint age 76 → 28% | yes. +10.8%, confirm −5.4%, 4-run mean +7.7% |
| 6-8 | io_capacity 1000 / 4000 / 8000 | refuted: −1.8 / −12.2 / −35.1% against the sweep base | yes. Raising flushing on a 97%-busy disk cannot help; only lowering it was plausible |
| 10 | doublewrite OFF (trade-off) | "improved-other-mechanism" | **wrong label**. "Waiting: data file I/O" includes page-cleaner writes, so more throughput meant more write wait. The mechanism did hold (doublewrite writes averaged 7.9 ms) |
| 12 | O_DIRECT_NO_FSYNC | refuted, +1.9% | yes |

### Measurement

- **Not steady in every run.** The history list grew by about 85-100k per 5 min with `innodb_purge_threads=1`. Calibration flagged the warm-up as inadequate, but `adaptive_duration=off` made that a warning only.
  - This is **purge lag that scales with throughput**, not a warm-up problem: it is flat in the slow change_buffering=none arm.
  - No purge experiment ran (Battery 1 M12 again).
- The change buffer sat at its 5% cap (`ibuf` about 75k pages) in every run. `innodb_change_buffer_max_size` was never a candidate.
- **Within-run noise estimate exceeds the run-to-run CV**: 9.4% within against 1.8% between. It fell to 0.45% with doublewrite OFF. It measures doublewrite-induced throughput oscillation, not sampling noise (section 8, R3).
- Swap: about 930 MB of mysqld memory in swap, 3-60 pages/s swap-in, labelled "swap-cold, not a problem".
- Six measurements of the same start configuration across #122, #125 and #130 (23190 … 21833) give CV 2.2%, so levels are comparable across sessions.

### Change-buffering A/B

**#130 (steady, ABAB, data reset plus 12-min warm-up per run):**

| Arm | inserts/s | Disk I/O per trx | BP hit | iowait |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| all | 21,833 / 22,797 | 10 | 99.96% | 20% |
| none | 2,695 / 2,778 | 106 | 94.8% | 65% |

- **change_buffering=none is −87.7%.** Without buffering, every insert or delete reads the leaf pages of 3 secondary indexes on a 59 GB set with a 23 GB buffer pool.
- The "all" arm is not fully steady (purge lag, change buffer at its cap), but the size of the effect makes the direction certain.
- **Keep `all` for this class.** MySQL 8.4's default of none is very costly here.
- The engine's static "change buffering rarely pays off" text (engine.py:445) should go; the both-ways experiment (222f6b0) is the right design.

**#118 (older build, 2-min warm-up):** −22.8% (all 2852 vs none 2201, both arms falling 10-19% across runs). It measured the cold buffer-pool fill, not steady state: inserts/s 2.8k against 22k steady. **The 2-min-warm-up ibench results (#106, #118) should not feed experience priors.**

## 3. #124 YCSB-A and #132 TATP (945b4ca)

### #124 YCSB-A

- **What limited it:**
  - At defaults: I/O-bound (hit 94%, 9.6k reads/s, free-page waits).
  - After the baseline: mostly CPU. 72% busy, data-file I/O wait 19%, redo/commit CPU 15%, network 14%, 61 running threads on 12 CPUs, run-queue wait 74%.
  - No I/O kind fired, yet I/O mattered: doublewrite OFF cut device await 2.08 → 1.31 ms and gave +12.4%. The real kind is **read latency inflated by write traffic**, and the classifier has no such kind.
- **Without evidence:**
  - the dirty_pages_pct_lwm sweep (3 runs; matched through the "commit" tag on redo CPU);
  - change-buffer-off (the change buffer was empty: `ibuf_size` 1);
  - redo-grow (no flush pressure);
  - best-practices;
  - huge pages, which **OOM-killed mysqld at start**: the rule reserved BP + 192 MB on 31 GB with only a 1 GiB `mem_avail` guard.
- **Hypotheses:** right except
  - ps-off "improved-other-mechanism": its target was the unprofiled "CPU (not profiled)" bucket (fixed by c0c4a7f / 582aa0e);
  - redo-grow "moved": targeted time −31% per trx, goal −7.9%, but the run was not steady (checkpoint age 19.9 → 31.6%), so no verdict should have been given.
- **Measurement:**
  - A 120 s warm-up was correctly detected as unsteady and redone at 240 s (+5.5%).
  - Throughput still rose 4-9% inside every 240 s window. `steady_state` only flags falling throughput.
  - Host swap-in of 64-121 pages/s, probably the BenchBase JVM (mysqld swap 0), was diagnosed as database memory over-commit.
- **Wrong decisions:**
  - The neutral best-practices bundle was never reverted and is the reported best safe configuration (Battery 1 M2).
  - The thread pool never ran: relevance 0-1 because sched-delay was excluded in this build (likely fixed by b410768; re-verify).
  - trx_commit=2 was not reached.
- **Settings:**
  - Mattered: doublewrite OFF +12.4%; PS OFF +3.0% on top.
  - Hurt: LWT OFF −7.2%; redo 32G −7.9% (unsteady).
  - No effect: BP +2 GB, dirty lwm, change buffering.

### #132 TATP

- **What limited it:** CPU 99.6%, 197 running threads on 12 CPUs, run-queue wait 96%. **mysqld used only 61% of the host CPU; the co-located BenchBase JVM used the rest.** The diagnosis said so, and nothing acted on it.
- **Without evidence:**
  - The read_ahead_threshold sweep (4 runs). Its "why" reads "Waiting: data file I/O takes **0%** of database time". It was admitted by the counter-based io-read diagnosis plus `acted_on` (any component with threads > 0). **Open at HEAD.**
  - change-buffer-off (empty buffer).
  - doublewrite OFF (exploratory).
- **Measurement:**
  - A step in disk reads from run 5 on (670 → 1,050 reads/s, also on start-config re-checks; 4 GB read per run on a 613 MB database).
  - It flipped 4 efficiency verdicts (runs 5, 7, 9, 10: "efficiency-loss" from disk I/O per trx alone).
- **Wrong decisions:**
  - LWT OFF (run 6): +1.99% against a 2.0% default threshold, with −4.6% mysqld CPU per trx. It was reverted with a wrong reason ("did not reduce CPU"), and not re-judged when the threshold fell to 0.87% (Battery 1 M8).
  - The trade-off best keeps doublewrite OFF for +0.03%: an efficiency-gain "kept over" edge. Battery 1 M4 (minimal trade-off set) is still open.
  - The log says "no value beat the parent … parent kept", then "sweep point run 11 improved: measuring again". The confirm correctly re-judged it as neutral.
- **Settings:**
  - Mattered: PS OFF +5.1%; LWT OFF about +2% with −5% CPU per trx but p99 33 → 57 ms.
  - Not tried: the thread pool, the obvious candidate.

## 4. #123 JOB (945b4ca, 2 vCPU, budget 8)

- **What limited it:** about 60% CPU / 40% data-file reads per stream (1.05 thread-equivalents).
  - **No CPU profile is ever taken** for run-to-completion benchmarks: `_maybe_profile` and `_maybe_offcpu` return when `warmup_s < 30`, and job.ini has warm-up 0. **Open at HEAD.** Without it the evidence gate cannot work for JOB, TPC-H or other single-run analytics.
- **Experiments:** 4 of 8 runs had no evidence (5 noatime, 6 subquery_to_derived, 7 join_buffer 8M, 13 AHI).
  - subquery_to_derived fired on `Max_execution_time_exceeded > 0` alone, but JOB has no correlated subqueries (engine.py ~602).
  - join_buffer: temp-file I/O time was 0, and its concurrency came from `threads_running` = 3.0 for one stream.
  - **AHI ON: +88% slower (2234 s, 16 capped queries).** Its gate (`bp_hit > 98`) fired on a read-bound run with 1800 disk reads/s, despite a track record of 0 holds in 4.
- **Sweep:** read_ahead_threshold 8 / 32 gave −2.7 / −2.9% (faster), judged against an inflated 5% threshold. The best configuration's 3 repeats spread only 0.09%, so this is probably a real ~2.8% gain that was missed.
- **Measurement:**
  - Calibration slid −2%/run: buffer-pool warming after the baseline restart (1244 → 1221 → 1196). That set the noise to 5% (2.5 × 2.0%).
  - d8efa67's drift check needs ≥ 4 runs, so it would not catch a 3-run calibration slide.
  - The 60 s query cap makes the total insensitive: 3 capped queries are 15% of the goal.
  - Only `job_total_s` was judged; geomean and capped count were collected but not used.
- **Harness:** a false "WARNING after reset: optimizer_switch is …" because a full switch string is compared with a partial one (cli.py ~1014, should use `knowledge.flags_subset`).
- **Missing rules for an optimizer-bound workload:**
  - **histograms**: 0 on the host; the bench's own histograms.sql calls them the main source of bad JOB plans. The stats-histograms advice needs one statement > 10% of time; JOB's maximum is 5.1%.
  - optimizer_search_depth / prune_level, eq_range_index_dive_limit, hypergraph.
- **Settings:** buffer pool −52%; AHI +88% (harmful); forced BKA/MRR +6.2% (harmful); read-ahead threshold low probably −2.8%; the rest neutral.

## 5. Battery 2 contention C1-C3 (16 vCPU server, remote 8 vCPU client)

- **The client is the limit or close to it.** C3 was `client-bound (high)` in every run (client CPU 97-100%). C2 was at 74-84% and became client-bound after trx_commit=2. C1 was at 70-75%.
  - With PS OFF in C3, server CPU per trx fell 7% while qps rose only 1.3%.
  - **C3 results are client-capped. The client needs ≥ 16 vCPU.**
- **Network/protocol is the top server CPU component** (30-44% of samples) in all three, and no candidate acts on it.
- **C2's 12% threshold is one harness outlier.**
  - Run 15's sysbench summary was 3,135 tps while the server's in-window tps was 3,750. The goal regex reads sysbench's **whole-run** summary (`--time` = warm-up + run + 30 s), and throughput collapsed in the last 50 s (2396, 94, 307 … 0 tps).
  - The robust estimator (a39ad3d) now caps the effect at ~3.5%. **The goal is still not cross-checked against the in-window server throughput.**
- **C2's best safe is −3.3% against the start.**
  - LWT OFF had an efficiency-gain verdict at run 6, then was **regressed** in its own re-test (run 13, −1.55%, noise 1.23%).
  - Under the inflated 12-15% threshold it tied with the start and won the efficiency tie-break. Today's `best_config` still picks it.
- **Other wrong decisions:**
  - Regressed run 13 was not reverted before run 14 (parent [13]).
  - The best configuration wandered: rollback to run 4 → 5 (a single neutral run) → 12.
  - Run 17's parent was wrong: best-practices was silently re-applied.
  - trx_commit=2 (+12.5%) was recorded as an "efficiency-gain" because the threshold was 13%.
  - In C3, neutral max_connections=1200 was kept in both final configurations.
- **Thread pool at 1024 connections:**
  - **C3: never generated.** The gate needs `threads_running_avg > CPUs`, and sampled Threads_running was 12-14 while ~500 threads waited in the OS run queue (runq wait 97%, 4.8 ms delay). **Open at HEAD (engine.py ~484).**
  - **C2: ranked 7-10 the whole session** (prior −11% from other classes) and ran only at run 24, on top of PS OFF in the trade-off phase. Result: −0.9% throughput, but threads running 463 → 50, scheduling delay 4.96 → 0.44 ms, history list 905k → 80k. It was never re-measured on the best safe configuration (Battery 1 M5).
  - **C1 (64 connections): −12.4%.**
- **History list in C2:** 750-900k with LWT ON against 80-150k with LWT OFF or the thread pool. Purge never became a candidate (score 1).
- **C3 thread_cache_size=256 "+1.45% confirmed" is not causal.** Threads_created = 0 with persistent connections. It came from the LLM, so the rule-id precondition did not apply.
  - **Open at HEAD:** `collector.analyze_window` drops counters with a zero delta (`y != x`). `_never()` then sees "not measured" and returns None, so **zero-counter preconditions (thread cache, table cache, …) can never fire.**
- **C1 (older build):**
  - Wrong parents: the tc sweep was compared with the regressed best-practices run, and sweep points with the previous point. Fixed by 5204bc4 / 483b417.
  - Start-config drift of −3.2% at run 8, then all configurations drifted up about 3-4%, so the +4.3% trade-off result compares late runs with early ones.
- **LLM:** again proposed `ps:profile` and `rg:innodb_bg_cpus` in C1 and C3 (Battery 1 L1 still open).

## 6. Battery 2 contention C4-C6 (5204bc4)

- **C4 hot rows: the row-lock bottleneck was never diagnosed.**
  - 9,000 row-lock waits/s at 14-15 ms is about 130 thread-equivalents, roughly 57% of the 233 running threads.
  - The `locks` rule needs `tps > 0`, but `tps = Com_commit + Com_rollback` is **0 for autocommit statements**; the log prints "tps 0.0". **Open at HEAD** (collector.py:93, engine.py:231).
  - HEAD's breakdown would show row-lock time, but the diagnosis-based paths still miss it.
- **C4 concurrency limiters all cut lock time and all lost throughput:** thread pool −24%, LWT OFF −13%, thread_concurrency 32 −44%, spin 10 −8%.
- **C4 redo-grow** (32G, +2.4%) was the only safe winner: row-lock wait 14 → 6.5 ms, flushed pages −54%. It ranked last in the safe phase.
- **Never proposed for hot rows:** `innodb_deadlock_detect=OFF`, `innodb_sync_array_size`, `innodb_spin_wait_delay`. There is no rule for any of them.
- **C5 TPC-C 256 VU:** the thread pool gave **+4.8%** and cut row-lock time 49 → 1.4 thread-equivalents, but was judged neutral against the calibration-inflated ±6.3% with a single-run parent. It was **never re-judged** when the threshold fell to 4.07% (Battery 1 M8).
  - LWT OFF: +1.9%, with redo 60 → 19 MB/s and fsyncs −82%. That is the opposite of C4.
  - redo-grow (the C4 winner) never ran: the trade-off phase started at 11 of 16 runs.
  - The safe item client-no-slow-start-idle was measured on the PS-OFF configuration (Battery 1 M5).
- **C6 connection storm: client-bound on every run** (8 vCPU at 99.9%, about 120 ms of client CPU per event, mostly the TLS handshake; mysqld at 1.2% CPU). The engine still ran 16 runs.
  - The 11% noise comes from a bimodal client (60.5-61.3 against 64.2-66.3).
  - LLM sweeps without evidence:
    - thread_cache_size, claiming "most connections miss the cache" while Threads_created was 0.003/s;
    - tcp_max_syn_backlog, with 0 SYN drops.
  - stack-small-wins combined two in-noise points and got −5.8%.
  - skip_name_resolve fired on connection rate (fixed by 71ad2d8 / bdf07a0).
  - The server-side 8.2% retransmit "network loss" diagnosis was a late-ACKing saturated client (client side 0.002%).
- **Huge pages:** the restart failed in all three sessions (and OOM-killed mysqld in #124).
